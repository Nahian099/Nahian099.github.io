Projects

Multimodal ML/DL/LLM Pipelines for Genotype, Local Ancestry, and SDOH in Asthma Prediction · Aug 2022 - Jul 2026

University of Tennessee, Knoxville

  • Multimodal deep learning (X-Admix). Designed and built a framework that fuses genotype, local ancestry, and SDOH through pairwise cross-attention and a learned softmax-gated fusion layer, capturing conditional cross-modal interactions that additive models miss. Reached AUC 0.771 on a low-signal asthma prediction task in an admixed African American cohort, where classical baselines stayed near chance (AUC 0.566 or lower). The performance trend held on a second, independent All of Us cohort roughly 3x larger.

  • Explainable AI for biological discovery. Layered feature ablation, gradient-based attribution, and SHAP with a leave-one-component-out protocol. Designed a two-tier consensus strategy that ranks candidate cross-modal interactions by cross-fold reproducibility, separating a robust, ablation-stable core from a larger discovery pool. Showed that a component can be redundant for accuracy yet still determine which interactions the model surfaces.

  • Pharmacogenomic treatment response prediction. Integrated genotype and local ancestry in a pediatric asthma cohort (N = 248, about 5 million features) to predict inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) response. Combined Lasso, Recursive Feature Elimination (RFE), and Elastic Net feature selection with expert priors; the stacked Lasso and Random Forest model beat unimodal baselines by 5 AUC points, and ablations showed the modalities carry non-overlapping signal.

  • Genomic language models (completed as part of doctoral dissertation, 2026). Compared four serialization schemes for encoding genomic variant data for pretrained large language models (LLMs) under a fixed token budget in an admixed All of Us cohort. Compressed encodings outperformed verbose prose descriptions (AUC 0.71 vs. 0.63). Designed an embedding-injection scheme that extends to unseen variants without adding parameters.
